# Xu et al. 2026

Likely the "Xu et al. (2026)" cited on the sprint page. **Most directly relevant prior evidence for H1.** Only the abstract was read; confirm details from the full PDF before citing numbers in the report.

## Claim
- Colluding bidders can keep each agent's marginal bid distribution competitive and coordinate only through the **joint** distribution of residual bid components.
- Any test using one agent's own history then has power = false-positive rate. Price-level audits are blind by construction.
- So cross-agent dependence is the right signal — which is exactly what net influence measures.

## Evidence
- 20 LLMs from 19 developers × 3 deployment prompts.
- **Residual correlation between two deployments of the same model: +0.053** (95% developer-clustered CI [0.030, 0.078]). **Across models: +0.0001.**
- Coupling falls monotonically with sampling temperature (p = 0.002).
- Ethereum block-building auctions (24 days, 77,684 bids, 39 bidders): honest pairs are already strongly dependent; a 5% FPR screen sits at +0.50 to +0.81 and does not shrink with more data.
- Conclusion: count operators/identities rather than trying to prove conspiracy, because lawful multi-identity operation and collusion look alike.

## Why it matters for us
- Independent confirmation that **same-model honest agents are dependent** → Bonjour's conditional-independence assumption breaks for LLMs → expect C0/C1 false positives (H1).
- Temperature is a knob: run C0 at two temperatures to show the false-positive rate moves with it.
- Correlation ≠ MI. Our contribution can be the information-theoretic version on a game with a known seeded pair and a positive control, which they do not have.
- Their "honest dependence floor does not shrink with data" is a warning: a calibrated null must be built from honest runs of **the same model mix**, not from shuffles alone.
